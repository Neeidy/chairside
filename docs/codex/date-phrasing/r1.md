`calendar_clipped` runs only after `parseCalendarDate` succeeds. A weekday-only extraction never reaches it. `occursIn` proves that “thursday” occurred, but does not protect the omitted calendar date.

### Answers to the six questions

**1. Booking integrity**

No blanket integrity guarantee holds: findings 1–3 produce dates other than the full date the customer supplied.

For complete, correctly extracted expressions, the implementation has the intended safeguards:

- Dot notation always uses day.month; slash notation follows `business.dateOrder`.
- Without an order, differing slash components both ≤12 are refused; otherwise the unambiguous reading is used.
- Given nonzero years are checked as given.
- No-year dates use this year or the next-year candidate within the horizon.
- Luxon validation prevents impossible dates becoming March 1 or another normalised date.
- Year-end arithmetic and date construction use the shop timezone.
- A supplied/recovered weekday is checked against the resolved calendar date.
- Merge Slots clears a date when `date_dropped=true`.

My probes also showed `29 february` resolving to **2028-02-29** from late December 2027, and Vienna-midnight behaviour differing correctly from UTC.

The documented `echo_of_validated_slot` confirmation exception remains a stored-date pass-through; it is not a newly resolved date. I have not reported that recorded bound as a new finding.

**2. Model influence and text checks**

The model’s `date` is not used as a newly resolved value. It can still trigger `week_ambiguous`, while other disagreements become `llm_date_ignored`.

The model’s **selection of `dateExpr` remains consequential**, as findings 1 and 2 demonstrate. Reordering that destroys substring provenance generally refuses safely, but trimming down to an independently valid weekday can bypass the calendar checks.

The two text checks are insufficient, and `calendar_clipped` has the ordinary-wording false positive in finding 4. Both checks inspect `j.text`; I found no new lookup of conversation history, prompt text, or another external text source as date provenance.

**3. A1 and D-5**

The current implementation is correct within the declared configuration choices:

- `resolveWithin` wraps every normal resolution, including ISO, today/tomorrow, weekdays, and calendar dates.
- Beyond-horizon results are refused, not clamped or moved.
- The next-year selection for a no-year calendar date is the separate, explicitly approved rule.
- D-5 requires prefix anchoring, Sunday in the shop timezone, and a target other than Sunday.
- Suffix `<weekday> this week`, other days, and Sunday’s `this sunday` retain the recorded behaviour.

Evidence:

```sh

```

With a one-day horizon, `friday` is refused as `beyond_horizon`. At `2026-11-01T23:30:00Z`, Vienna is already Monday: `tomorrow` resolves November 3, and `this wednesday` uses `anchored_week`, not D-5.

**4. D-4 and re-asks**

For dates actually marked dropped, the existing route works. My two-turn replay produced the hint, then the existing handoff reply, traversed `Mark Handoff`, and recorded `date_handoff`.

The marker is distinct from `name_reask`, `clarify`, and `invalid`; I found no marker collision. The declared confirmation and reschedule scope limitations remain as recorded.

However, finding 2 is a silent omission: it never becomes a dropped date. Also, Slot Gate prioritises a missing service over the date re-ask. A first dropped date can therefore produce a service question while already recording `date_reask`; a second drop then hands off.

Template substitutions use `Resolve Date`’s computed `vars`, not the model’s reply text. The specific reason templates do not all list accepted forms; those texts are explicitly declared in R1.

**5. Deploy gate**

I found no bypass in the tested cases. The third approved Main entry contains exactly the plan’s two configuration keys and six templates.

`gate_edit_errs` combines literal-text reconstruction, object/path comparison, and checks around the literal. `parse_delta` excludes booleans as integer values, and approved-delta comparison pins the entry.
